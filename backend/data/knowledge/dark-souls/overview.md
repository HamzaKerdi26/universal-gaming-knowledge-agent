# Dark Souls

Dark Souls is an action role-playing game developed by FromSoftware,
first released in 2011 as a spiritual successor to the studio's earlier
game Demon's Souls. It is widely credited with establishing the "Souls-like"
genre and popularizing a design philosophy built around high difficulty,
sparse guidance, and a strong sense of earned progress.

The game is set in the kingdom of Lordran, a decaying world where fire —
the force that originally brought light, life, and distinct beings into
existence — is beginning to fade. The player takes on the role of an
Undead who has escaped confinement and sets out to either rekindle the
First Flame or let it die out, depending on the choices made near the
end of the game.

Dark Souls is known for its interconnected level design, in which
distinct areas loop back and connect to one another in ways that
gradually reveal a coherent map, often rewarding careful exploration with
shortcuts back to earlier checkpoints. Death is a core mechanic: dying
causes the player to drop their accumulated currency at the location of
death and reduces their maximum health until it's recovered — or lost for
good if the player dies again before reaching it.

The game's minimal hand-holding, combined with methodical, high-stakes
combat, became a defining influence on a large number of later action
RPGs from many different studios.
